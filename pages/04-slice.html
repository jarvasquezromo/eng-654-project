Lost motion: right singular vector of σmin

Elbow curve: \(q_3 \approx -77^\circ\)

The lost motion lives almost entirely in \((q_2,q_3)\): the lever arms of joints 2 and 3 to the wrist line up. For the positional subchain at \(O_4\) (intersection of axes 4 and 5) this happens exactly at \[q_3^* = -\tfrac{\pi}{2} + \operatorname{atan2}(0.110,\,0.470) = -76.83^\circ\] (dashed line). The other root, \(103.17^\circ\), and its \(-256.83^\circ\) twin fall outside the joint-3 limits \([-225^\circ, 85^\circ]\), so GoFa has a single reachable elbow separator. The full-robot curve sits near \(q_3^*\) but moves with \(q_4,q_5\) (about \(-100^\circ\) to \(-55^\circ\)) because of the 0.080 m wrist offset. Try the wrist sliders.

Shoulder curves

Here the lost motion is dominated by \(q_1\), typically paired with an equal and opposite \(q_4\) or \(q_6\). The base axis and a wrist roll axis become (nearly) aligned through the tool, so turning the base can be undone by the wrist. This is the offset-wrist counterpart of the wrist centre crossing the base axis. Inspect a blue point to see the pairing.

Wrist-coupled curves

The lost motion is mostly \(q_4\)–\(q_6\), mixed with \(q_1\). For a spherical wrist this is the clean \(\sin q_5 = 0\) set. For GoFa, axes 4 and 6 can be parallel without being collinear, so \(q_5=0\) alone is not singular: at home, \(\kappa \approx 35\). The wrist singularity becomes a curve that depends on \(q_2,q_3\).

The colours come from the dominant block of the null vector at each sign change of \(\det J\). The classification is computed, not assumed. Check any point in Inspect.

What is evaluated

For each grid cell, the full tool Jacobian \(J(q)\) from the screw model (Step 3). Linear rows are scaled by \(1/L\), then the singular values come from a Jacobi eigen-solve of \(J^\top J\).

Singular curves

Cells where \(\operatorname{sign}\det J\) changes against a neighbour. A sign change is a robust zero crossing, unlike thresholding \(\sigma_{\min}\). Each is classified by its right singular vector \(v_6\), using the energy in \(\{q_1\}\), \(\{q_2,q_3\}\) and \(\{q_4,q_5,q_6\}\).
