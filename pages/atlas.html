Singular curves and IK counts

Left: \(D(q_2,q_3)=\det J_p\) and its zero set. Right: their images, the critical curves in \((\rho, z)\). Tap either plot to solve IK; numbered markers are the solutions.

Standard D–H, \(^{i-1}T_i = R_z(q_i+\theta_{i,0})T_z(d_i)T_x(a_i)R_x(\alpha_i)\). The point is the frame-3 origin moved by d₄ along z₃ (d₄ = 0 for the lecture presets; 0.470 m gives GoFa's wrist point O₄). D does not depend on q₁ or d₁.

Found numerically: points of the singular curve where the kernel of the meridian map becomes tangent to the curve, confirmed by checking that the image chords on both sides leave the critical value in the same direction. For the offset preset they match the lecture's table (q₂, q₃) = (±129.78°, 66.39°), (±78.60°, −171.93°).

Regions and counts

Critical curves split the section into regions; inside one region every target has the same number of regular IK solutions (shaded, with the count written in it). Crossing a fold curve, two solutions merge and disappear.

Cusps

At a cusp three solutions coalesce. A target path that loops around a cusp's critical value can bring the robot to a different IK solution without meeting a singularity: the robot is cuspidal. The a₁ = 0 preset has none; the a₁ = 1 preset has four.

Link to GoFa

The GoFa preset is GoFa's real position subchain (q₁, q₂, q₃ → wrist point O₄). It has intersecting first axes (a₁ = 0, d₂ = 0), no cusps and the classic 4/0 count: arm branches only change at the elbow and shoulder singularities, which is why the elbow line q₃* = −76.83° bounds the arm branches in Steps 4–7. Full GoFa has an offset wrist (a₅ = 0.080 m), so its 6-D IK (up to 16 solutions) is not wrist-partitioned and is not captured by this 3R picture.

What is not included

Joint limits and collisions are ignored here, as in the lecture atlas: every count is the number of mathematical (regular) solutions on the full torus. Steps 5–7 apply GoFa's limits.

Joint path vs. workspace loop

Tap two IK markers that share a colour region in joint space (same sign of D, same aspect): a path between them avoids singularities only if the robot is cuspidal.
