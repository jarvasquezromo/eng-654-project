Step 4 · Topology of GoFa's IK

Singular curves and IK counts

The Lecture 06 atlas for GoFa itself. Vertical tool: exact, with joint space on the four sheets of solutions and the workspace on \((\rho, z)\) or any plane. Other tools (Tool tab): numerical map of the plane. Tap any plot to solve the full 6-joint IK.

Orientation (tool z axis = approach)

R = Rz(yaw) Ry(pitch) Rx(roll), the same for every point of the plane.

Tool length (TCP offset along tool z)

The map point is the TCP; the flange (tool0) sits ℓ behind it along the tool axis. det J does not depend on the reference point.

Why the tool axis is vertical

With \(z_6 = \mp\hat z\), turning the base by \(q_1\) only spins the tool about its own (vertical) axis, which \(q_6\) undoes. So the IK count and the singularities depend only on \(\rho = \sqrt{x^2+y^2}\) and \(z\): the same axisymmetry the lecture uses for its 3R atlas. (For a tilted tool this symmetry is lost; use the task-space plane view for that.)

Four sheets

The tool axis is \(z_6 = z_5\), and \(z_5\) is always perpendicular to axis 5 (\(z_4\)). So \(z_6 = v\) needs \(v \perp z_4\). With \(q_1 = 0\), \(z_4 = (\sin q_4 \sin s,\ \cos q_4,\ \sin q_4 \cos s)\), \(s = q_2 + q_3\), hence \(v\cdot z_4 = \mp\sin q_4\cos s = 0\). The solutions form four 2-D sheets: A \(q_4 = 0\) and B \(q_4 = 180^\circ\) (plotted over \(q_2, q_3\)); U forearm up, \(s = -90^\circ\), and D forearm down, \(s = +90^\circ\) (plotted over \(q_2, q_4\); there \(q_5 \in \{0, 180^\circ\}\), axes 4 and 6 parallel). On each sheet \(q_5\) is unique and \(q_6\) absorbs \(q_1\). Sheets meet along lines (A ∩ U is \(q_4 = 0,\ s = -90^\circ\), and so on). Every solver solution was checked to lie on one sheet.

Planes

Because the count depends only on \((\rho, z)\), the critical set in 3-D is the critical curves rotated about the base axis. A plane cuts those surfaces exactly: on xy at height \(h\) they become circles of radius \(\rho\) wherever a critical curve crosses \(z = h\); on xz at \(y = c\) (or yz at \(x = c\)) each critical point maps to \(x = \pm\sqrt{\rho^2 - c^2}\). Region counts on a plane are full IK solves at a point of each region.

Any tool

If the tool axis is not vertical, a base rotation also tilts the tool, so the count depends on the full position and the (ρ, z) picture does not exist. The page then solves the chosen plane point by point (in web workers): every pixel gets its IK count, boundaries where the count changes are drawn as the critical curves, and every solution is plotted at its \((q_2, q_3)\) on one of the two wrist sheets (sign of \(q_5\); the 4-5-6 wrist has exactly two solutions for a given orientation), coloured by det J. Dark points (det J ≈ 0) trace the singular configurations whose images are the boundaries. A yaw-only change keeps the axis vertical and stays exact.

Singular set

\(D = \det J\) of the full 6×6 Jacobian (it does not depend on \(q_1\) or \(q_6\)), evaluated on each sheet. Its zero curves are the singular configurations; their images are the critical curves. Every preimage of a target on either sheet is one full IK solution, checked against the solver: up to 16 for general orientations, here up to the count shown in each region.

Joint limits

q₁, q₂, q₄, q₅ have a full turn and q₆ more, so only q₃ removes configurations: q₃ ∈ (85°, 135°) (mod 360°) is outside [−225°, 85°]. With “Joint limits” on, that band is hatched (a horizontal band on A and B, a vertical band in q₂ on U and D, since q₃ = s − q₂ there) and counts only use feasible solutions.

Regions and counts

Inside a region between critical curves the number of IK solutions is constant. Crossing a fold, two solutions merge on a singular curve and disappear. This is what bounds the feasible placements in Step 7: a contour that crosses a fold loses the branch that was folding.

Cusps

Marked ◆ when found: three solutions coalesce there, and a target loop around a cusp can bring the robot to another IK solution without a singularity (cuspidal behaviour). The search checks every singular curve on both sheets.

Aspects

Red and blue areas are the two signs of det J. A path in one colour patch never meets a singularity. Solutions with the same sign on the same sheet may still be in different patches.
